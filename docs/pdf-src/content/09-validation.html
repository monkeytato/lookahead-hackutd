Section 6

Validation, reported honestly

Two kinds of check: the physics against published cooling-outage transients, and the statistics against a harness that knows the "true" outcome. Both are reported with their misses. Neither is yet a replay of a measured site incident, which is the next real validation.

6.1 Thermal backtest against WP179 and its CFD room

Procedure. Configure the model with WP179 Example 1's stated room (30 × 24 × 4 m plus a 0.6 m plenum, 400 kW, 100 racks of 545 kg, generator at 60 s, chiller restart at 12 min). Fit only the one declared unknown, the loop water volume, to the published 41.1 °C baseline peak. Then predict the 5-minute and 4-minute restart cases, the recovery time and the CFD room. The curves below come from running the shipped simulateThermal with the exact parameters of tests/engine/thermalZone.test.ts (kG = 2, ε = 0.8, instant chiller restart).

Reading the backtest.

  • Textbook coupling (kG = 1) over-predicts the late rise badly: 45.6 °C against 37.3 °C at 300 s in the CFD room.
  • kG = 2 meets the CFD criteria and the Example 1 baseline and 5-minute peaks.
  • It misses the 4-minute peak by about 2.5–3.5 °C. A one-node water loop cannot reproduce how fast a restart recovers; a two-node loop is the likely fix and is future work.
  • The fitted loop volume falls to the grid floor at kG = 2: . The loop volume is therefore not identifiable from WP179 and is sampled from practice instead (§3.1).

6.2 Are the intervals honest? Coverage

Harness. npm run validate runs 36 scenarios (6 named, 3 of them modelled on real incidents, plus 30 random) × 50 iterations, seed 1. Each iteration draws a "reality" and asks whether each realised loss time falls inside the predicted p10–p90. For an honest 80 % interval the answer should be yes about 80 % of the time. The default perturbation widens delays ±30 % with a per-iteration bias of ±20 %, shifts start times ±2 min, scales leak and spread rates ±40 %, and misses a first signal 20 % of the time (up to 5 min of extra detection lag).

What the three numbers mean.

  • {{covLegacy}} % before. The legacy model sampled only delay noise, so its intervals were far too narrow. By asset type, chiller coverage was 4 % and ATS 5 %; under the perturbed reality the ensemble lifts every asset type, to between 38 % (chiller) and 61 % (battery).
  • {{covPrior}} % self-consistent. When reality is drawn from the same priors, the ensemble's intervals contain {{covPrior}} % of outcomes, inside the 80 ± 3 % target. This is a correctness check of the weighting and the quantiles (a bug would show here), not a claim about the real world.
  • {{covPert}} % under widened priors. When reality departs from the priors (wider delays, shifted starts, different rates), coverage drops to {{covPert}} %, below the 75–85 % band. Coverage more than doubles, but the intervals are still too narrow when the priors are wrong, and the median error grows from 3.0 to 4.9 minutes.
Why the UI says "model range"
Until validated coverage lands in 75–85 % under the perturbed reality, the interval is not called a p10–p90. coverage.ts generates the UI note from the latest results: "{{coverageNote}}"

6.3 First-rack accuracy

"First rack right" asks whether the model names the rack that is actually impacted first. It rose from {{frLegacy}} % of {{judgedLegacy}} judged iterations (legacy) to {{frPert}} % of {{judgedPert}} under the perturbed reality and {{frPrior}} % of {{judgedPrior}} under self-consistency. Predicted versus realised racks saved by a fix was checked only for the legacy model, which predicted 32.1 racks saved on average against 23.6 realised, an overstatement of about 36 %. The ensemble runs did not check fixes ("0 scenarios checked"), so fix accuracy for the new engine is not measured.

6.4 Engine tests that guard the science

Prior-predictive coverage within 72–88 %; evidence-posterior calibration on synthetic truth (each P(real) bin within ±0.05); no foreknowledge (forecasts invariant to unobserved future faults, a property test); bounded importance weights, a rare event recovered within 3 SE at N = 8,000 and unbiased random proposals; EVSI ≥ 0 and equal to brute force on a toy case; the thermal backtest, energy conservation and closed form against Euler. 282 tests passed at the end of the overhaul.
